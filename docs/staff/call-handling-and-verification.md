> Sample procedure written for an academic project. It is not a Kenya Airways internal document.

## When to use

At the start of every call, before you discuss or change a booking.

## Before you start (what to check)

- The caller's booking reference (PNR) or e-ticket number.
- Whether the caller is the passenger, the booker, or a travel agent.

## Steps

1. Greet the caller and give your first name.
2. Ask for the booking reference or e-ticket number.
3. Verify the caller against the booking: full passenger name as on the ticket, and one more detail held on the booking (contact email or phone, or the travel date and route).
4. If the caller is not the passenger, confirm they are the booker or that the passenger has authorised them. Only discuss what is needed for the request.
5. If verification fails, don't read out any booking details. Offer general policy information only, and invite the passenger to call back or use Manage Booking.
6. Record the verification method in the booking remarks.
7. Handle the request using the matching procedure (refunds, rebooking, baggage, special assistance).
8. Before ending, summarise what was done and any reference numbers.

## What to tell the customer

- Why you are asking verification questions: to protect their booking and personal data.
- Never ask for a full card number, card security code, password or one-time code.

## Escalate if

- The caller cannot be verified but insists on changes.
- The caller is abusive or threatening.
- The call involves a data request (access, correction, deletion). Refer it to the privacy contact in the Privacy Policy.

## Based on (public policy and section)

- Privacy Policy: personal data handling and data subject rights.
- Conditions of Carriage: who may change or refund a ticket.
